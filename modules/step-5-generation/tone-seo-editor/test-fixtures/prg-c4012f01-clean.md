## [Overview] Pocket Rockets Gaming — RGS Platform Builder and iGaming Consultancy

Pocket Rockets Gaming builds Remote Gaming Server (RGS) technology and works as an iGaming consultancy for operators and B2B platforms, advising on platform decisions [#7]. Its flagship offering, Engage, is a modular product suite centred on the Engage RGS, described as a multiplayer-first Remote Game Server built to run both multiplayer and single-player games from a single core [#7]. The company positions crash, bingo, poker and PvP formats as first-class use cases rather than add-ons, alongside conventional single-player game support [#7].

Alongside its platform, the company runs a consultancy practice covering technical and product due diligence, game porting, and market-entry advisory for operators, studios and, per its directory listing, regulators [#1][#9]. Engage is offered through a partnership-led model rather than a generic SaaS arrangement, with the company reporting it is working with studios and operators to co-develop and operator-brand titles while seeking further strategic partners [#7].

Pocket Rockets Gaming describes itself as a boutique consultancy with a scope spanning casino, slots, bingo and sportsbook advisory work [#1]. It operates from a small team and, as an early-stage platform builder, discloses no public licences, awards or named commercial partnerships to date — a limitation relevant to operators assessing its market track record. Its combination of proprietary RGS platform development and advisory work distinguishes it from single-purpose game studios or pure consultancies in the same space [#7][#9].

## [Primary Category: software-development-services] Engage RGS and Platform Engineering

Pocket Rockets Gaming's core business is iGaming software development: building bespoke Remote Gaming Server technology for studios and operators through its Engage product suite [#7]. Engage RGS is described as a modern, modular platform designed for real-time performance and deterministic concurrency handling, with seamless RGS API integration into external wallets and platforms [#7]. The core separates game logic from platform concerns, keeping game engines lightweight and portable, while the RGS core itself manages transactions, sessions, RNG and orchestration [#7].

The company positions its platform development work as suited to several distinct project types:

- Launching new titles directly on the Engage core [#7]
- Porting existing games from legacy RGS platforms onto Engage [#7]
- Adding social or competitive layers to previously single-player games [#7]
- Scaling game operations across multiple markets [#7]

Beyond the core platform, services include iGaming consulting and advisory, technical and product due diligence, game porting and API integration, and bespoke or operator-branded game development [#7]. These services are described as existing specifically to support studios and operators adopting Engage or modernising an existing platform [#7]. The company also publishes process-improvement guidance for casino operations and game integration teams, attributing common delivery bottlenecks to fragmented processes and unclear ownership between technical, product and operations functions [#10].

## [Primary Category: strategy-consulting] Build-vs-Buy and RGS Selection Advisory

As a strategy consulting practice, Pocket Rockets Gaming advises operators and game studios on one of the sector's recurring platform decisions: whether to build a proprietary RGS or license an existing one [#9]. As an iGaming consultancy for operators and B2B platforms, the company's evaluation considers over 30 distinct aspects of a candidate RGS platform, grouped around ease of game build, ease of operator integration, operational costs and available features [#9].

On the technical side, the company's due diligence approach examines what tooling a platform provides for independent front-end and back-end development, the functionality of its Game Development Kit (GDK), and whether a platform supports game replay for regulatory purposes [#9]. On integration timelines, the company notes that operator or aggregator integration work typically takes one to four months once scheduled, and that licensing an already-integrated RGS can shorten time to market compared with a ground-up build [#9].

The company also describes a "try before you buy" SaaS route, in which a studio's existing game front-end can be paired with a hosted back-end at relatively low upfront cost, letting studios begin building and distributing games within months before deciding whether to license the underlying source code [#9]. This same build-vs-buy and technical due diligence discipline underpins its Engage-related consulting services.

## [FAQ] Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: What is an iGaming platform?**
An iGaming platform, or Remote Gaming Server (RGS), hosts casino games and manages functions such as random number generation, transactions and regulatory compliance behind the scenes [#9]. Pocket Rockets Gaming builds this kind of infrastructure through Engage, positioning it as a multiplayer-first core that also supports conventional single-player titles [#7].

**Q: Build vs buy for an iGaming platform — which makes more sense?**
Pocket Rockets Gaming advises that licensing an existing RGS often gets studios to market faster than building from scratch, since integration into aggregators and operator platforms can take one to four months regardless of platform origin [#9]. The company evaluates over 30 aspects of a candidate platform — build ease, integration effort, operating cost and features — before recommending a direction for a given client [#9].

**Q: How does a crash game API integrate with poker software?**
Both formats run through the same Engage RGS core, which separates game logic from platform concerns so different community formats share transaction, session and RGS API integration handling [#7]. Crash, poker and bingo are treated as first-class multiplayer formats within this shared architecture rather than as separately engineered systems [#7].

**Q: Why is my gaming platform so slow under load?**
Performance and scaling problems in gaming platform consulting engagements typically trace back to how an RGS platform's architecture handles concurrency and load, an issue Pocket Rockets Gaming says it has helped clients diagnose and resolve as part of its due-diligence and advisory work [#9]. The company also links integration delays to organisational factors, citing fragmented processes and unclear ownership between technical and operations teams as a common root cause [#10].

**Q: Who needs gaming platform consulting?**
Operators and studios evaluating whether to build, license or migrate a gaming platform are the core audience, alongside teams facing performance, scaling or integration bottlenecks [#9]. Pocket Rockets Gaming's own positioning extends this to regulators and cross-functional teams, describing itself as a boutique consultancy spanning technical, product and compliance advisory across casino, slots, bingo and sportsbook [#1].
